                      Reading this data

                      Seven things shape how these numbers should be quoted. Please read them before this dashboard travels any further.

                      The Direct anomaly, in one chart

                      Grey shows Direct sessions. Blue shows the Direct sessions where somebody actually engaged with the page. If Direct were a real audience, the two lines would move together.

                        1. Direct traffic needs a source investigation

                        Direct sessions went from 27,158 in January to 896,817 in September, peaking at 931,957 in July. Across all nine months, engaged Direct sessions stayed between roughly 4,300 and 6,500, average engagement time per session fell from 9.4 seconds to 0.4 seconds, and almost all of the volume landed on the homepage. The user data agrees independently: Direct first-touch users went from 27,494 to 909,321 while average engagement time per active user fell from 10.3 seconds to 0.44 seconds.

                        The pattern starts in February, not March. February is already down to 4.6 seconds per active user, so January is the last clean month and the right baseline for this pilot.

                        What to check on your side: bot and internal traffic filtering in the GA4 property, then any uptime monitor, security scanner, load balancer health check or synthetic test hitting the homepage. Something changed in late January or early February and the timestamp should be findable.

                        Source: GA4 Traffic Acquisition and User Acquisition exports, Jan to Sep 2026

                        2. AI figures before June were rebuilt, not reported

                        Google Analytics created the AI Assistant channel group for this property in June 2026. Pull a raw channel report for January through May and it will show zero. The figures in this dashboard for those months were rebuilt from the referring domain in the Landing Page report, counting chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com, vertexaisearch.cloud.google, perplexity.ai, copilot.com and claude.ai.

                        That method checks out. For June and July, where both views exist, rebuilding from the referring domain produces 141 and 136 sessions, which matches Google Analytics' own AI Assistant channel totals exactly. The reconstruction is sound, and it is still worth saying out loud whenever the January to May numbers are quoted.

                        The equivalent user figures cannot be rebuilt. The User Acquisition export carries no referring domain, so AI users before June show as not available rather than zero. They were not zero, they were ungrouped.

                        Source: GA4 Landing Page export with Session source / medium, cross-checked against Traffic Acquisition

                        3. Sessions and users answer different questions

                        The channel and page views count sessions and credit the channel that drove that specific visit. The audience view counts people and credits the channel that first brought them to the site, ever. Somebody who found you through Google in March and came back through ChatGPT in July appears under organic search in one view and AI search in the other.

                        Both are correct. They are simply not comparable channel by channel, so please avoid placing a user figure and a session figure for the same channel side by side.

                        Source: GA4 Session primary channel group vs First user primary channel group

                        4. Two counting rules worth knowing

                        Google Analytics removes duplicate people, so new users plus returning users will not add up to total users in any month. January Direct shows 27,494 total, 26,824 new and 1,037 returning. Adding the last two together produces a number that does not mean anything.

                        Google Analytics also caps each monthly landing page export at roughly 1,000 rows, so the deepest long tail is cut off. Page totals therefore run between 0.1% and 0.5% below the site totals. Use the channel view for anything site-wide.

                        Source: GA4 export behaviour, observed across all seven monthly pulls

                        5. Rank tracking has six days of history

                        The keyword section runs on SE Ranking, not Google Analytics, and that project started on 21 August 2026. Six daily checks exist. Position changes across those six days are normal SERP fluctuation, so please treat the section as a baseline snapshot and not as a performance trend.

                        Search volumes come from SE Ranking's United States database and are national figures. A geo-modified term such as "home care agency warrenville il" often reports zero volume while still driving qualified visits, which is why zero-volume terms stay in the tracked set rather than being pruned.

                        Source: SE Ranking project "Addus SEO", site 12802157, first check 21 August 2026

                        7. Six days of keyword data were removed, 25 to 30 September

                        SE Ranking stopped returning positions correctly on 25 September 2026. Ranking keyword counts collapsed on that date across every project on the Digital Reach account, including clients with no connection to Addus, so this is a tool fault and not a change in any website's performance.

                        Four things establish that. Addus keywords holding position 1 to 3 sat at 92 every single day for five weeks, then read 81, then 30, then 9. JourneyCare's Google map pack positions held steady at 43 to 47 every day while its organic positions fell from 90 to 11, and the map pack and the web results come from the same page of search results. Arcadia Home Care held 47 to 70 ranking keywords for eleven consecutive days and then dropped to 4. Ahrefs shows the same websites essentially flat across the identical window.

                        A manual export from the SE Ranking interface shows the same collapse as the automated feed. That rules out a reporting fault and places the problem in the position checking itself, which is what the support ticket needs to say.

                        What this means here. Section 07 runs to 24 September 2026, the last clean day, and the six affected days are excluded rather than charted. Every other section comes from Google Analytics, Search Console or Ahrefs and is current to 1 October 2026.

                        Source: SE Ranking, four projects compared 29 September 2026 and a manual interface export of 1 October 2026, cross-checked against Ahrefs Site Explorer

                        6. Four tools, four different questions

                        Google Analytics counts sessions and users on the site. Search Console counts what Google showed and what people clicked before they arrived. SE Ranking checks positions on a fixed keyword list. Ahrefs estimates traffic and authority from its own crawl and click model.

                        They will never agree, and they are not supposed to. Ahrefs estimates about 4,500 monthly organic visits where Google Analytics recorded 17,902 organic sessions in September. Both are correct for what they measure. Quote each figure with its tool attached and never put two of them in the same comparison.

                        The competitor set in section 08 was built by hand from per-market SERP work, because Ahrefs' automatic competitor list for addus.com returns biography.com and a blood bank. That is a symptom of the heritage content problem, not a tool fault, and it is worth knowing before anyone re-runs the report.
